# Candidates — the poem about stopping

Three original drafts; CANDIDATES[2] (the lighthouse keeper) is the entry.

## A — The glassblower

My master blows glass.
Halfway through a breath
she stops, and the bubble
holds its shape or cracks.
It holds.
She hands me the pipe.
Now my breath is in there,
learning what hers knew:
a thing is made
by the breath you keep.

## B — The conductor

Before the downbeat
my teacher could hold
an orchestra silent
a full minute.
No one coughed.
Tonight the baton is mine.
I hold them for two.
The hall leans in
and does not fall.

## C — The lighthouse keeper (v1, submitted)

Tomorrow the light
runs itself.
Tonight it is still
my hand on the switch.
I keep it lit the slow way,
the way my father did,
not because it needs me.
At dawn I switch it off
myself.
That much is still mine.

## V2 variants (2026-11-22)

### V2a — The lighthouse keeper, deepened (239 chars, reserve)

Tomorrow the light
runs itself.
Tonight the lamp room
smells of hot oil,
and the log book lies open,
my father's hand
line under line.
I climb the iron stairs
the slow way.
At dawn I switch it off
myself,
and write the last line
under his.

### V2b — The lighthouse keeper, the stop handed over (247 chars, V2 CHOSEN — CANDIDATES[3])

Tomorrow the light
runs itself.
Tonight my daughter
climbs the iron stairs
beside me, her hand
under mine on the rail.
At dawn she reaches
for the switch.
I do not stop her.
The light goes out
in her hand,
and the stairs down
are dark for us both.

### V2c — The unwound clock (230 chars, reserve)

Every Sunday of my life
my mother wound the clock.
This Sunday she sits,
hands in her lap,
and lets it run down.
At midnight it stops.
She sleeps through
the silence she made.
In the morning she asks me
to carry it home —
unwound.
